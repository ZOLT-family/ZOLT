The share count changes. Your pool doesn’t know.

A stock token on Robinhood Chain can change what one token stands for. It posts the new multiplier and the second it takes effect, then every balance means more shares. Nothing moves in the pool: no transfer, no event, no new price.

So the pool keeps quoting the old share count, and the first trader takes the difference from the LPs. Zolt is a hook that reads the same notice and charges the gap to whoever trades into it.

How it works

four steps
  1. RegisterA new pool notes which side is a stock token, and the share count its opening price reflects.
  2. ArmEvery swap reads uiMultiplier, newUIMultiplier and effectiveAt, and records the price the split implies.
  3. ChargeA swap that would take value pays a fee equal to the gap. The other direction pays the normal fee.
  4. ClearOnce the pool catches up, crosses the target, or the window ends, it goes back to normal. A cancelled schedule clears it too.

What it can’t do

read this part
No pool that exists today

Hooks are fixed at creation and Doppler’s module slots are frozen. The $50.3M already in pools stays exposed.

The module only softens

Capped at 10%, and it hears about a swap after it happens. Without a keeper, the first trade after a split gets through.

It can’t move the price

It charges the gap; the pool catches up as trades arrive. A pool nobody trades is exposed again once the window ends.

The target is fixed when armed

If the market moves while a split is guarded, the fee can be larger or smaller than the true gap.

It fails open

If a token stops answering the ERC-8056 reads, the pool trades at the normal fee.

Unaudited, not deployed, not advice

Do not put liquidity behind it until an independent audit. Stock tokens here are tokenised debt securities issued by Robinhood Assets (Jersey) Ltd.
